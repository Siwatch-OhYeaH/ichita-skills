capability pillars as stated on the profile: **engineering design & turnkey project**,
**research & development**, **water treatment**, and **process solution**. Group
company: **S.K.G.21 Co., Ltd.** Operates the **ICHITA Pilot Center**.

Positioning line used on the closing slide: *"**WE** listen to your challenging problems,
and develop solutions for our customers."*

## Technology map

Four technology families, feeding four process outcomes:

- **Chromatography (SMB)** — sugar derivatives: IMO, FOS, DRM, Allulose
- **Membrane separation** — liquid sugar process, brine recovery, concentration
- **Adsorbent (Ecosorb / Nuosorb / Nuosep HP)** — decolorization
- **Resin (ion exchange)** — demineralization, decolorization, brine recovery

Product streams handled: glucose, maltose, fructose 42/55, organic syrup, maltodextrin,
liquid sugar, sweetener syrups.

## ICHITA Pilot Center

**Pilot lineup:** mixing-tank reactor with temperature control · fixed-bed reactor ·
leaf filter · decolorization unit · demineralization unit · ultrafiltration unit ·
membrane unit · SMB chromatography. Plus a **mobile SMB chromatography plant** (containerised).

**Development capabilities**
- *Sweetener production* — glucose, maltose, fructose, IMO syrup: liquefaction &
  saccharification → filtration & demineralization → fructose conversion & purification
- *Syrup decolorization* — Nuosep HP; Ecosorb / Nuosorb
- *Ultrafiltration separation* — yeast extract, pineapple peel syrup, resistant
  maltodextrin decolorization, raw sugar / molasses decolorization
- *Sugar derivatives separation by SMB / membrane* — resistant maltodextrin purification,
  IMO purification, isomalt purification, brix recovery / sugar concentration
- *Allulose production*, 8 steps — prepare fructose syrup → enzyme conversion → Ecosorb
  filtration → demineralization → chromatography separation → brix recovery → sheet
  filtration → evaporation

**Pilot result on record — IMO-900 purification by SMB chromatography**
(sugar profile %, four runs against feed)

| Sample | Glucose | IG2+IG3+P | IMO content |
|---|---|---|---|
| IMO-500 feed | 32.0 | 26.2 | 69.3 |
| IMO-900 #1 | 8.1 | 53.0 | 92.3 |

Achieved: **IMO content > 90%**, **glucose < 2%**, IMO profile (IG2+IG3+P) **> 45%**.
IG2 = isomaltose, IG3 = isomaltotriose, P = panose.

## The sugar-derivatives argument (TSMC seminar)

The strongest narrative ICHITA has, and a model for the deck arc in `README.md`.

**1. What the industry faces** — fluctuating world sugar price, near-lowest domestic price,
rising production cost, climate change, competing crops. Then demand-side pressure:
beverage sugar tax, health concern, risk of non-sugar sweeteners. Thai figures quoted:
**20.8 million** overweight, **3.3 million** living with diabetes, **300,000** new patients
per year.

**2. What to do** — sugar derivatives. Higher product value; the factory sets the price;
global demand rising with consumer trends; health benefits; industry profit up.

**3. Value creation chain** — cane → mill → raw sugar → refinery → fine liquor → **liquid
sugar plant** → **liquid sugar derivatives plant** → derivative products (MIS; FOS /
oligofructose / lactosucrose; allulose / tagatose; isomaltulose / isomalt; erythritol /
sorbitol / mannitol).

**4. Liquid sugar process, with the specification travelling with the stream**

| | Fine liquor | Liquid sugar |
|---|---|---|
| Colour | 150 IU | < 35 IU |
| Ash | 0.15% | < 0.05% |
| Brix | 50–55% | 67% |

Train: Ecosorb decolorization → demineralization → taste & odour polishing →
sterilization → evaporation.

**5. The commercial case — operating cost per ton DS**
- Liquid sugar route: **1,000–1,500 THB/ton DS**
- Refinery crystallization route: **7,000–9,000 THB/ton DS**
- Beverage-maker remelt route: **1,500–2,000 THB/ton DS**

Claimed gains: more effective use of sugar · energy saving · much lower production cost ·
refinery yield up **1–2%** · opportunity for derivative products.

**6. What sugar derivatives are** — four families off sucrose/glucose/fructose:
basic sugar · sugar alcohol (sorbitol, maltitol, erythritol, isomalt) · sugar fibre /
prebiotic (FOS, lactosucrose) · rare sugar (allulose, tagatose, isomaltulose, trehalulose)
· amino sugar (D-glucosamine, N-acetyl glucosamine).

**FOS** — sucrose → transfructosylation → kestose (DP3) / nystose (DP4) /
1-fructosyl-nystose (DP5). Sugar-like sweetness and solubility; prebiotic and dietary
fibre. Market (2022): total value **2,600 M**; **57.8%** of prebiotic sugar is FOS;
**70.8%** of FOS is produced from chicory root.

**Allulose (D-psicose)** — rare sugar found in jackfruit, raisins, wheat and maple syrup;
made by **30% conversion** from fructose. Approved in USA, Japan, Korea, Mexico, Colombia,
Chile; EU novel-food approval pending; China approval expected early 2024 *(as stated in
the deck — check current status before reuse)*.

Contact on that deck: **siwatch@ichitathailand.com** — Siwatch Chomchai, product
development.
