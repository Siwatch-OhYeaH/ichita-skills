Presentation method · specimen 02

The technical teaching deck

The Ecosorb webinar sells one product to a room that already has the problem. This second specimen — Liquid Sugar & Derivatives Technology, 57 slides, internal and customer technical training — teaches a whole capability. The arc is the same skeleton; five moves are specific to teaching, and they are what this card records.

Specification blocks, not tables

Every stream carries the same five-line block, in the same order, at both ends of the train. Before → after is read across the drawing. The output block gains lines as the deck goes deeper; the input block never changes.

One check per slide, nothing removed

Three consecutive slides, identical drawing. Each adds one line below the last, and the money is derived on screen from the two figures already shown — never asserted as a total.

Move 04 · analogy before schematic

Explain it in the world first

The hardest idea in the deck — how a fixed bed simulates a moving one — is taught with a rabbit and a tortoise on an airport walkway, one slide, before any port diagram appears. The mechanism slide that follows is then just labelling.

speed of walkway > speed of rabbit > speed of tortoise → slow-moving group and fast-moving group separate

Use the analogy only for the mechanism the audience cannot picture. One per deck, never for something they already know.

Move 05 · motion by repetition

Five identical slides, one step each

The SMB cycle is animated by duplicating the slide five times and advancing the ports one position per copy. Layout, labels and scale are frozen; only the coloured ports move. It survives PDF, print and a projector with no build support.

Orange (Attention) = extract port · Blue = feed port. Same rule as the carried diagram: one change per slide.

Two columns, trait against trait, symmetric length, no colour and no recommendation. The engineer in the room picks; the deck's job was to make the pick possible. State the confidentiality mark — For ICHITA internal training only — on the slides that carry unpublished pilot data, not on the deck.

Bookend the section with its own map. The derivative-possibilities tree opens the section and closes it, identical both times — the second viewing is the recap.
Name the presenter. Cover carries name and role under the title; a training deck is delivered by a person, not by a company.
Every reference is a name, a duty and a capacity. Five plants on a grid, then one slide each for the flagship with its location.
State the design brief as a numbered list, then close it with a single italic thesis line the whole section has to satisfy.
Derive, never assert. Two figures on screen and the arithmetic between them beats one big number.
Close with the ask. Thank you, then the feedback slide. The last thing on screen is what you want the room to do.
