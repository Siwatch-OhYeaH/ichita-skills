Secondary & functional palette

Final · settled 4 August 2026 · supersedes all earlier drafts and reference sheets

Two different jobs, two different sets. Functional colour says what state something is in — within spec, pending, off-spec. Secondary colour says which of ICHITA's four technology families it belongs to. Confusing the two is what makes a data slide unreadable, so the palettes are built and named separately and share no hue. Together: eight colours, and the list is closed.

Fill and type are different steps

Every hue splits in two. The core is a fill — bars, chips, rules, chart series, 3:1 graphics bar. The text step is for type, 5.6:1 or better on white. Never set type in a core; never flood a field with a text step. This one split is what makes the set easy to use.

85 / 15

Primary neutrals and Ichita Blue hold ~85% of any layout; every other colour on it, added together, stays under ~15%. If a surface is more than a sixth coloured, it has stopped being an ICHITA surface and become a generic corporate template.

Subordinate by chroma

Ichita Blue is oklch chroma 0.215. No secondary exceeds 0.135; Bronze, a neutral, is 0.036. Set any of them beside the accent and the accent still wins the page — which is what “one vibrant accent” has to mean once there is more than one colour.

Functional — four states, drawn through slate

The system previously shipped the stock Material triad: #34A853, #E83E3E, #FFA000. Three screaming, unrelated hues that made every ICHITA table look like a Google console. These are the same states with slate mixed in — calmer, cooler, of a piece with Blue Grey, and every one of them more legible than the colour it replaces.

Secondary — four families, four hues

One job: telling the reader which technology they are looking at. The mapping is fixed company-wide — once a reader learns cyan = membrane on slide 6, it must still be true in the datasheet, the quotation and the chart. The product stream deliberately has no colour of its own: liquid sugar is the output of all four families, so colouring it sets it competing with them.

Guardrails

Do

  • One secondary per surface. A slide about membranes is cyan; it does not also carry bronze. Two category hues on one slide and the reader cannot tell what is being distinguished.
  • Keep the mapping fixed across every document ICHITA produces.
  • Reach for grey first. If nothing on the surface needs distinguishing, there is no secondary colour on it.
  • Use the ground tokens so field and type are chosen together.

Do not

  • Never on the cover, the closing slide, the letterhead or the logo. Those surfaces are the identity itself — Blue Grey 03 and Ichita Blue only.
  • Never use a secondary as a status, or a functional colour as a category. They share no hue precisely so this stays impossible.
  • Never put Signal Orange in a status key, and never beside Oxide Red.
  • Never set type in a core, and never add a hue that is not on this card.

Closed questions — do not reopen

The functional colours are now branded, and that was the biggest win available. Replacing the Material triad cost nothing and improved all three: Oxide Red measures 4.38:1 on white against the old 4.05, Technical Amber 2.22 against 2.04. They are calmer and more legible.

No additional blues. “Water Blue”, “Tech Blue” and “Process Cyan” were measured and cut. Tech Blue #1D57C8 is hue 262 — Ichita Blue's own hue at 86% of its chroma; it is the accent darkened, and already ships as --ich-blue-text. Nothing within 40° of hue 262 may enter the palette. Cyan sits at 204, a clear 58° away.

No “Graphite”. #5B6770 is hue 240 at chroma 0.021 — that is Blue Grey. The brand already owns three greys; a fourth under a new name is a maintenance liability.

No second red, green or orange. Industrial Red, Sage Green and Safety Orange all duplicate a functional colour under a category name. That overlap is exactly what makes a data slide unreadable — a green bar meaning sustainability in the legend and within spec two rows down.

Ice Cyan and Coral Rose are in, at working lightness. Both were proposed at values that cannot hold a label — #32C5D2 is 2.09:1 on white and #D96B78 is 3.32:1. Their character is kept: Ice Cyan is now --ich-cyan-light, correct on dark grounds and as a fill; Coral Rose became the Rose ramp, pushed from hue 14 to 350 so it can never be misread as Oxide Red.

Eight is the ceiling — four states and four families. ICHITA has four technology families. A coding system only pays for itself if the audience can hold it in their head; past about seven or eight they stop decoding and start guessing.
