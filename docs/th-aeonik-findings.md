# TH Aeonik ten-weight build — findings

Record of what was measured while extending TH Aeonik from 6 faces to 20, what
broke along the way, and what the results mean for using the fonts. The build
reference lives in [th-aeonik-weight-scale.md](th-aeonik-weight-scale.md); this
document is the evidence behind it.

Specimen sheets are in [specimens/](specimens/), regenerated by
`python3 scripts/make_specimens.py`.

---

## 1. The sources are not an interpolatable master set

The goal needed ten weights; Aeonik ships seven and Bai Jamjuree six, so the
gaps had to be interpolated. That only works if corresponding glyphs have
matching point structures — and they do not. Comparing adjacent weights:

| Pair | Glyphs with identical point structure |
|------|----------------------------------------|
| Bai Jamjuree ExtraLight → Light | 28 / 87 |
| Bai Jamjuree Light → Regular | 38 / 87 |
| Bai Jamjuree SemiBold → Bold | 17 / 87 |
| Bai Jamjuree ExtraLight → Bold | 4 / 87 |
| Aeonik Light → Regular | 203 / 323 |
| Aeonik Bold → Black | 199 / 323 |

These are static instances, not masters. Each was exported independently and
the per-weight curve optimisation dropped different redundant points from each.

**But contour *counts* match essentially everywhere** — the mismatch is point
counts *within* contours, differing by 1–3. The designs are topologically
identical. That is what made the whole approach viable: outlines that agree on
topology can be brought into structural agreement without redrawing anything.

## 2. Compatibilisation is lossless

Subdividing a Bézier at any parameter with de Casteljau produces two segments
that trace exactly the original curve. So point counts can be equalised by
splitting, never by moving points. `interpolate_masters.py --self-test`
asserts it by checking that the blend at t=0 reproduces master A and at t=1
reproduces master B:

```
Aeonik CFF   compatible 280/315   max deviation t=0: 0.0017u  t=1: 0.1684u  [LOSSLESS]
Bai TTF      compatible  79/87    max deviation t=0: 0.0108u  t=1: 0.1300u  [LOSSLESS]
```

On a 1000-unit em that is at most 0.02% — far below rasterisation resolution.
Structural compatibility went from 63%/8% to 100% before the quality gate in
§4 trimmed it back to the figures above.

Building that test honestly took three attempts. Early runs reported ~300 units
of error at t=1, which was the *metric* measuring its own artefacts: contours
get rotated and reordered during compatibilisation, and a coarse reference
polyline cuts corners. The measurement had to become invariant to start point
and contour order, and refine against the curve rather than its samples,
before it measured the outlines instead of itself.

## 3. Three defects found by verification

None of these were visible in the build logs; each was caught by rendering or
by an independent check.

**Composite glyphs were silently emptied.** `RecordingPen` records composites
as `addComponent` references rather than decomposing them, so the outline
extractor saw zero contours and wrote a *blank glyph*. `BoundsPen` decomposes
automatically, which is why bounds looked correct. Every accented Latin glyph
and every precomposed Thai mark cluster was affected — visible as `น้ำ`
rendering as `นา`, having lost both its tone mark and the nikhahit. Fixed by
using `DecomposingRecordingPen`, plus a guard that refuses to emit an empty
glyph when the master has ink.

**Node correspondence was drifting.** Matching nodes by arc-length position
pairs them wrongly when a heavier master distributes its length differently.
Interpolation hides this — the endpoints are still exact — but extrapolation
doubles every mis-pairing. It showed up as collided Thai at 800/900, with `ด`
extrapolating to xMin −39 where linear prediction says 50, and dipping 53 units
below the baseline. Replacing arc-length matching with an order-preserving
geometric alignment brought worst-case bbox deviation from ~89 units to 7.

**Counters were filling in solid.** Structural compatibility is not the same as
*correct* correspondence: pair a round counter with a rotated copy of itself
and every node lines up, yet the midpoint interpolates visibly smaller. The
percent sign's lower counter blended to 60% of its correct area and rendered
as a filled disk. Two separate causes sat underneath — contours stored in
different order between masters (Aeonik's `oe` has its counters swapped between
Light and Regular), and start points no rotation could reconcile.

## 4. The quality gate, and why the obvious threshold was wrong

Each contour pairing is now checked by blending it and comparing the enclosed
area against what a faithful blend would enclose. A pairing that fails is
rejected, and the glyph keeps a master's outline instead of shipping distorted.

The first version compared against the *mean* of the two master areas and
rejected 20% of glyphs. That target was wrong: **area is quadratic in the
coordinates.** Blending circles of radius 1 and 2 gives radius 1.5, enclosing
2.25π — not the mean 2.5π. The correct expectation is the midpoint of the
square roots, which predicts a well-corresponded contour to within a fraction
of a percent (40883 predicted vs 40879 measured on the percent sign's good
counter, against 24680 for its bad one).

Error across Aeonik's Light→Regular pairs runs smoothly with no natural cliff —
80% of contours under 2%, trailing off to 24% — so the 12% threshold is a
judgement call: it clears the visible defects and spares the mildly imperfect
accents that make up most of the tail.

Glyphs that fail fall back to the master **nearest in weight**. That detail
matters more than it sounds: falling back to master A would have put 19 common
Thai letters (ข จ ช ต ม ย ล ส อ and the tone mark ້) at Medium 500 inside a
~710 face. Falling back to the nearest master puts them at Bold 700, which is
visually seamless.

Resulting fallback rates, over glyphs each face actually ships:

| Face | Fell back |
|------|-----------|
| Latin Book 350 | 57 / 473 |
| Latin SemiBold 600 | 71 / 473 |
| Latin ExtraBold 800 | 44 / 473 |
| Thai Book 350 | 8 / 87 |
| Thai ExtraBold / Black | 19 / 87 |

## 5. Bai Jamjuree cannot leave its shipped range

The hardest limit found. Measuring where outlines begin to self-intersect —
the failure mode that actually bounds extrapolation, since Thai letters are
built from small loops that close up and cross:

| Target weight | Factor | Thai glyphs self-intersecting |
|---------------|--------|-------------------------------|
| 900 | +2.00 | 40 of 86 |
| 850 | +1.75 | 17 of 86 |
| 800 | +1.50 | 8 of 86 |
| 740 | +1.20 | 3 of 86 |
| **730** | **+1.15** | **0 — last clean step above Bold** |
| **200** | — | **0 — lightest clean weight** |
| 150 | −0.25 | 11 of 87 |
| 100 | −0.50 | 33 of 87 |

At 900, `ธ` was destroyed outright and nearly half the alphabet crossed itself.
So Air 100 uses ExtraLight outlines unchanged and the two heaviest steps are
damped to a verified-clean ~710/720. **The Thai flattens at both ends of the
scale while the Latin runs the full 100–900.** Widening it needs a Thai face
with masters outside 200–700, not a change to this pipeline.

Note the counter-collapse check reported all of this as "clean" — a glyph can
self-intersect while every contour keeps a plausible area. Only an
intersection test finds it, and only as a *delta* against the masters, since
type designers routinely leave contours overlapping (they fill identically
under the non-zero winding rule).

## 6. Vertical metrics need both a floor and a measurement

Replacing the family's hardcoded `usWinAscent = 1550` with measured ink was a
regression, caught by the repo's own `verify-fonts.py` failing all 20 faces.
Measured ink is too small because **GPOS stacks a tone mark above a vowel, so
a cluster reaches higher than any single glyph's bounding box** — no per-glyph
measurement can discover the space it needs.

The floors alone are also too small: Black's descenders reach −570, past the
historic 561. The build now takes `max(floor, measured ink)`, floors 1550/561.

## 7. Bold and Italic in Word / PowerPoint

See [specimens/word-bold-italic.png](specimens/word-bold-italic.png).

| Family in the font menu | Ctrl+I | Ctrl+B | Ctrl+B+I |
|---|---|---|---|
| **TH Aeonik** | real | **real Bold 700** | **real Bold Italic** |
| The other eight families | real | **faux** | **faux** |

**Cause.** Ctrl+B does not mean "make this heavier". It means "switch to the
Bold slot of my current family". OpenType's legacy naming gives a family
exactly four slots — `nameID2` may only be Regular, Italic, Bold or Bold
Italic — and a font *file* occupies exactly one (family, slot) pair. Ten
weights cannot fit in four slots, so eight of them were given their own family
names, each holding only Regular + Italic:

```
TH Aeonik             filled=4/4   missing: none          <- Ctrl+B works
TH Aeonik SemiBold    filled=2/4   missing: Bold, Bold Italic
TH Aeonik Black       filled=2/4   missing: Bold, Bold Italic     ...and six more
```

With the Bold slot empty, Word has nothing to switch to and smears the outline
instead. Only `TH-Aeonik-Bold.otf` carries `nameID2 = "Bold"` and the
`head.macStyle` BOLD bit; that pairing is the entire mechanism.

Putting all ten in one family is not an alternative — 20 faces would compete
for 4 slots and most of the family would become unreachable.

The synthetic result is worst at the extremes: at Air and Thin it thickens
strokes crudely and lands near Regular, discarding the design; at ExtraBold and
Black it barely changes weight at all (the smear is a small fixed amount) and
merely muddies the counters.

**Guidance: pick the weight by name from the font dropdown and leave the B
button off.** The exception is TH Aeonik itself, where Ctrl+B on Regular is
genuinely correct.

This affects Word and PowerPoint only. InDesign, Illustrator and Figma read
`nameID16`/`nameID17` — which every face carries, and which has no four-style
limit — so they show a single "TH Aeonik" with all ten weights in the style
menu.

**Optional alternative.** The weights could be style-linked into five RIBBI
quartets so every Ctrl+B lands on a real face (Air→Light, Thin→Book,
Regular→Bold, Medium→ExtraBold, SemiBold→Black). The cost is that the font menu
drops to five names and Light, Book, Bold, ExtraBold and Black stop being
selectable by name. Not done — for a brand font, weights being pickable by name
matters more. It is a naming-only change if that judgement ever flips.
